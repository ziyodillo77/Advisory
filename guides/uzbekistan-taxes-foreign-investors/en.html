• Uzbekistan has some of the lowest headline tax rates in the CIS: 15% profit tax, 12% VAT, 12% flat income tax.
  • Dividends paid abroad are taxed at 10% at source, often reduced to 5% under a tax treaty.
  • Small companies can pay a simple turnover tax instead of VAT and profit tax.
  • IT Park and the new TIFC offer 0% corporate tax regimes for qualifying activities.

Since the tax reform of 2019, Uzbekistan has cut rates sharply, simplified reporting and moved almost everything online. For a foreign investor the system is now predictable, but the choice of regime and the way profits are paid out make a large difference to the final tax bill. Here is an overview of the rules in force in 2026.

2. Turnover tax for small businesses

Companies whose annual revenue is below the threshold set by the Tax Code can pay a turnover tax, generally 4% of revenue, instead of VAT and profit tax. It is simple and works well for service businesses with low costs. Once revenue passes the threshold, the company moves to the general regime: 15% profit tax plus 12% VAT.

The turnover tax is not always cheaper. If your margins are thin or your customers are VAT payers who want input VAT, the general regime can cost less. We compare both before registration.

4. Double tax treaties

Uzbekistan has tax treaties with more than 50 countries, including Russia, Kazakhstan, the United Arab Emirates and Turkey. Treaties usually reduce withholding tax on dividends, interest and royalties, and protect against paying tax twice on the same income.

To use a treaty rate, the foreign recipient must give the Uzbek company a certificate of tax residency from its own country before the payment is made. Without it, the full domestic rate is withheld and has to be reclaimed later.

5. Tax residency of individuals

A person who spends 183 days or more in any 12-month period in Uzbekistan becomes a tax resident and pays 12% on worldwide income. Foreign directors and employees who relocate should plan for this, especially if their home country also taxes them. TIFC investment residents can be exempt on foreign-source income.

6. Special regimes

  • IT Park: 0% profit tax on qualifying IT income, 7.5% income tax for employees and a 1% contribution from revenue, guaranteed until 2040.
  • Tashkent International Financial Centre: exemption from profit tax, VAT and social tax on qualifying financial services, and 0% income tax for foreign staff, until 2076. See our TIFC guide.
  • Free economic zones: tax and customs relief for manufacturing projects, depending on the zone and the size of the investment.

7. Compliance in practice

  • Tax returns are filed electronically on the tax authority's portal (soliq.uz), signed with the company's digital signature.
  • Electronic invoices are used for sales between businesses, and VAT credits depend on them.
  • Financial statements are prepared under national accounting standards. Banks, insurers, listed companies and other large entities also report under IFRS and need an audit.
  • Profits can be repatriated in foreign currency after taxes are paid.
