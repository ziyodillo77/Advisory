• 100% foreign ownership is allowed in most sectors. No local partner is needed.
  • No minimum charter capital for a standard limited liability company (LLC).
  • Registration itself usually takes 1–2 working days once the documents are ready.
  • The real time goes into legalising foreign documents, the digital signature and the bank account. Plan for 2–5 weeks in total.

Uzbekistan has become one of the easiest countries in the region to open a company in. Registration is done online through a single government window, and foreign founders have the same rights as local ones. Below is the process we follow for clients from the CIS, the Gulf and Europe, step by step.

1. Choose the right structure

Most foreign investors use one of three options:

  • Limited liability company (LLC, in Uzbek "MChJ"). The standard choice for trading, services, IT and investment holdings. It can have from 1 to 50 participants, and foreign individuals or foreign companies can own 100% of it. Each owner's liability is limited to their contribution.
  • Representative office. Suitable if you only want to promote your parent company, research the market or support partners. It cannot earn income. It is accredited by the Ministry of Investment, Industry and Trade for 1 to 3 years.
  • Branch or project presence. Possible for specific contracts such as construction. Activity of more than 183 days, or a fixed place of business, usually creates a "permanent establishment" that is taxed in Uzbekistan.

For financial firms, the new Tashkent International Financial Centre (TIFC) offers a separate regime with English law and long tax holidays. Export-oriented IT companies should also look at IT Park residency.

2. Prepare the documents

If the founder is an individual

  • Passport copy, notarised and translated into Uzbek or Russian.
  • A personal identification number (PINFL). Foreigners obtain it in Uzbekistan, and it is needed for a digital signature and online government services.

If the founder is a foreign company

  • A recent extract from the commercial register of its home country.
  • Its charter or articles of association.
  • A board or shareholder decision to set up the Uzbek company and appoint the director.
  • All of these must be apostilled (or legalised at a consulate if your country is not in the Apostille Convention) and translated with a notarised translation.

For the new company

  • The company name, checked and reserved on the government portal.
  • A legal address in Uzbekistan, usually an office lease agreement registered electronically.
  • The charter, the founders' decision and the director's details.
  • A power of attorney, if a local representative files the documents for you.

3. Register the company: step by step

  1. Reserve the name on the Unified Portal of Interactive Government Services (my.gov.uz). The reservation is held for a limited period.
  2. Sign the foundation documents: the charter and the decision to establish the company.
  3. Submit the application online, or at any Public Services Centre, and pay the small state fee.
  4. Receive the registration, usually within 1–2 working days. The company is entered in the state register and receives its taxpayer number (STIR) automatically.
  5. Open a bank account. Banks carry out their own checks on the owners and the director. Accounts can be opened in soums and in foreign currency.
  6. Pay in the charter capital within the period set by law and your charter.
  7. Choose the tax regime and register for VAT if required. See our tax guide for foreign investors.

4. Can it be done remotely?

Yes. With a notarised and apostilled power of attorney, a local representative can reserve the name, file the documents and receive the registration for you. Two steps are easier in person: obtaining your personal identification number and digital signature, and the bank's identity check. Many banks now accept video identification, so ask before planning a trip.

5. Visas and work permits

A foreign director or employee needs the right visa and, as a rule, a work permit issued by the Agency for External Labour Migration. The permit is applied for after the company is registered. Special regimes are simpler: IT Park residents can hire foreign specialists without quotas, and TIFC participants will be able to employ foreigners without separate work authorisation and obtain visas of up to five years.

7. Mistakes we see most often

  • Corporate documents that are not apostilled, or whose translation spells names differently from the passport.
  • An office address without a properly registered lease, which delays registration and the bank account.
  • Choosing the tax regime by default instead of comparing the turnover tax, VAT and special regimes.
  • Signing foreign-currency contracts without checking currency control and repatriation rules first.
