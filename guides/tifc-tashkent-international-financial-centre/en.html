• A special jurisdiction inside Tashkent with English common law, English as its official language and its own international commercial court.
  • Tax incentives guaranteed until 1 January 2076: no corporate income tax on qualifying financial services, 0% income tax for foreign employees, no VAT on those services.
  • Built for Islamic and investment banks, fund managers, insurers, brokers, fintech and family offices.
  • Operations are expected to start in 2027. Implementing rules are being published now, so this is the time to prepare.

The Tashkent International Financial Centre is the most ambitious investment reform in Uzbekistan's recent history. It follows the model of the Dubai International Financial Centre and the Astana International Financial Centre: a separate legal and tax environment designed to attract international banks, funds and investors to Central Asia.

1. Legal basis and timeline

  • 30 March 2026: Presidential Decree UP-48 set out the tax and customs incentives.
  • July 2026: the President signed the Constitutional Law "On the Tashkent International Financial Centre". The Constitution was also amended to allow special legal regimes and specialised courts.
  • Within 12 months: TIFC bodies adopt implementing regulations: boundaries, licensing rules, fees and lists of qualifying services.
  • 2027: the Centre is expected to start operations.

2. English law and its own court

Inside the Centre, the principles, statutes and court precedents of England and Wales apply directly, as long as they do not conflict with the Constitution of Uzbekistan, the Constitutional Law or the Centre's own acts. English is the official language of the Centre.

Disputes are heard by the Tashkent International Commercial Court, which has a first instance and an appeal level, can include foreign judges and works in English. Its judgments are enforceable throughout Uzbekistan. For foreign investors this means familiar contract law and predictable dispute resolution.

The Centre is governed by the TIFC Council, chaired by the President of Uzbekistan, together with the TIFC Administration, the Financial Services Authority (the regulator) and the court.

There are also exemptions for investors: gains on selling shares in TIFC participants or exchange-listed securities, and dividends and interest on such securities, are not taxed for companies or individuals (subject to the exchange criteria set by the Centre). Investment residents, wealthy individuals who become Uzbek tax residents through the Centre, are exempt on foreign-source income after paying a mandatory residency fee.

4. Who qualifies

The corporate tax exemption covers income from these financial services, together with related ancillary services:

  • Islamic banking.
  • Reinsurance and insurance brokerage.
  • Brokerage, dealer and underwriting services.
  • Investment management of fund assets.
  • Other financial services added by TIFC acts.

The Centre also licenses digital-asset businesses, payment services, fintech, green finance, and audit, legal and advisory firms. Digital-asset exchanges are licensed but excluded from the corporate tax exemption.

5. Conditions you must meet

  • Real substance: an office, qualified staff and management in the Centre.
  • A licence from the Financial Services Authority for regulated activities.
  • Separate accounting of exempt and non-exempt income and expenses.
  • Compliance with governance, anti-money-laundering and cybersecurity rules. Fines can reach USD 10 million or 10% of the value of the transactions involved.

6. Other benefits

  • Visas of up to five years for staff and their families.
  • Hiring foreign specialists without separate work authorisation.
  • Contracts and payments in any agreed foreign currency, and free repatriation of capital and income.
  • Guarantees against nationalisation and expropriation.

7. TIFC or IT Park?

IT Park is the better fit for software development and IT export: 0% corporate tax on qualifying income, 7.5% employee income tax, a 1% revenue contribution, guaranteed until 2040, under Uzbek law. TIFC is designed for regulated financial services and investment structures that need English law, an international court and incentives that last until 2076. Some groups use both: a TIFC entity for finance and an IT Park company for technology.

8. How to prepare now

  1. Map your activities against the qualifying service lists and model your tax position.
  2. Choose the structure: a new entity, a subsidiary of your group or a fund vehicle.
  3. Prepare the licence application, business plan and compliance framework for the Financial Services Authority.
  4. Plan substance: office, key staff, visas and bank accounts.
